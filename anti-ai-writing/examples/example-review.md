# Anti-AI Writing: Example Review

This is a worked example of the skill's active review mode, run on a short AI-sounding paragraph. The four sections below are exactly what the skill returns.

**The draft under review:**

> In today's rapidly evolving landscape, leveraging cutting-edge AI solutions can be a game-changer for organizations. It's not just about technology, it's about transformation. Honestly, the possibilities are endless, and the future looks bright.

## 1. Violations found

| Quoted text | Rule | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" | Rule 8, Rule 2 | Formulaic opener that delays the point |
| "leveraging cutting-edge AI solutions" | Rule 6 | Tier 1 vocabulary, "leverage" and "cutting-edge" |
| "a game-changer" | Rule 9 | Significance inflation with no measure |
| "It's not just about technology, it's about transformation" | Rule 20 | Parallel negative construction used for effect |
| "Honestly" | Rule 23 | Banned word, every instance is a defect |
| "the possibilities are endless, and" | Rule 30 | Comma placed directly before "and" |
| "the future looks bright" | Rule 15 | Generic, empty conclusion |

## 2. Rewritten version

> On-site inspection of bridge welds with a vision model cut the review time from four hours to twenty minutes in our pilot, because the model flags the joints a human should look at rather than every joint. The remaining work is the false-positive rate, which sits at eight percent and needs a second pass before the method replaces the manual review.

## 3. Change summary

The opener and the conclusion were deleted, since both delayed or padded the point rather than carrying information, which addresses Rule 2 and Rule 15. The inflated vocabulary was replaced with a measured claim, a cut from four hours to twenty minutes, which addresses Rule 6 and Rule 9. The parallel negative construction was dropped in favour of a direct statement, which addresses Rule 20. The banned word and the comma before "and" were removed, which addresses Rule 23 and Rule 30.

## 4. Second-pass audit

The rewrite was re-read for surviving patterns. It carries no Tier 1 vocabulary, no em-dash, no semicolon, no comma before "and" and no banned word. The two numbers each carry a unit and a context, so they pass Rule 13. The result reads as a specific claim a person would make, so the audit is clean.
